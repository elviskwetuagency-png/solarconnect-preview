Quick answer

For most solar systems in Kenya, lithium (LiFePO4) batteries are the better long-term buy. They can be discharged far more deeply each day, need no maintenance and, on the manufacturers' own figures, last several times as many cycles as gel batteries. Gel batteries cost less up front for small systems, but you can only use about half their capacity. Compared on usable energy, the upfront cost is surprisingly close, and lithium delivers far more energy over its life.

The battery is usually the most expensive part of a solar system after installation, and the one people most often regret choosing on price alone. Here is how lithium and gel batteries compare, using the specifications and prices of batteries we actually stock.

Usable capacity: the number that matters

A battery's label shows its total capacity, in amp-hours (Ah) at a voltage, or in kWh. How much of that you can use without damaging the battery depends on its chemistry:

  • The gel batteries in our shop list 50% depth of discharge. A 200Ah 12V gel battery holds 2.4 kWh, but you should only use about 1.2 kWh before recharging.
  • Our lithium batteries list much deeper discharge, with some Felicity models at 95% or more and Suness models rated at 90%. Planning around a conservative 80% still gives far more usable energy per kWh on the label.

This is why a "smaller" lithium battery often replaces a bank of larger gel batteries.

Cycle life and lifespan

A cycle is one discharge and recharge. In a solar home the battery cycles roughly once a day, so cycle life translates directly into years.

Maintenance, weight and heat

  • Maintenance: both gel and lithium batteries are sealed. Gel batteries are maintenance-free in the sense that you never add water, but they need to be fully recharged regularly to avoid sulphation. Lithium batteries manage themselves through a built-in battery management system (BMS).
  • Weight and space: lithium packs are much lighter and smaller for the same usable energy, which helps with wall-mounted or indoor installations.
  • Heat: all batteries age faster in high temperatures. In hot areas such as the coast, install batteries in a shaded, ventilated space. Check each battery's listed operating temperature range.

When gel still makes sense

Gel batteries are still a reasonable choice when:

  • the system is small (lights, TV and phone charging) and the budget is tight,
  • you need a short-term or temporary backup, or
  • you are replacing a battery in an existing lead-acid or gel system whose charger settings cannot be changed for lithium.

Our gel range runs from KSh 15,500 (Felicity 100Ah 12V) to KSh 34,000 (R Energy 200Ah 12V).

Choosing the right lithium battery

  • Match the voltage to your inverter: 12V, 24V (25.6V) or 48V (51.2V).
  • Size by usable kWh for your night-time and outage needs. Our guide to sizing a solar system walks through the maths.
  • Check inverter compatibility. Many lithium batteries communicate with hybrid inverters over CAN or RS485, so confirm your inverter supports the battery brand.
  • Plan for expansion. Several models can be connected in parallel to add capacity later.

FAQs

Can I replace gel batteries with a lithium battery on my current inverter?

Often yes, but the inverter must support lithium charging settings, and ideally communicate with the battery. Tell us your inverter model and we will confirm before you buy.

Can I mix old and new batteries, or gel and lithium?

No. Mixing chemistries, ages or capacities in one bank causes uneven charging and shortens battery life. Replace the whole bank, or add identical lithium modules designed to run in parallel.

How many years does a lithium solar battery last?

At roughly one cycle a day, a battery listed for 6,000 cycles is rated for over 15 years of cycling under test conditions. Real-world life is shorter in heat or with poor charging settings. Warranties on our lithium batteries are typically 3 to 5 years.

What is LiFePO4?

Lithium iron phosphate, the lithium chemistry used in most home solar batteries. It is valued for its long cycle life and thermal stability.
